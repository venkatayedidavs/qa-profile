2.8 Process, leadership and collaboration

Why this lesson is here. Process and leadership follow the technical blocks. The plan says not to answer strategy as a tool user only.

How do you decide what to automate?

Risk and repetition. Regulatory and money-path flows first, then high-frequency regression, then stable high-value journeys. Do not automate unstable features, one-off exploratory findings, or anything where maintenance cost exceeds the manual cost.

How do you introduce Playwright into a team that uses something else?

Start with a thin vertical slice on a single high-value journey; prove it in the pipeline; measure run time and failure-signal quality against the incumbent; keep both suites running during migration; migrate by risk order rather than alphabetically; and invest early in developer onboarding so the suite does not become one person's property.

A developer says your test is flaky and the feature is fine. How do you respond?

Be evidence-led rather than defensive. Open the trace together. It is often a real race condition users would hit occasionally, which turns the conversation into a shared defect rather than a test problem. If it is the test, fix it and say so plainly.

How do you work with developers on testability?

Negotiate stable test ids and accessible names as a contract; ask for seeding endpoints and feature flags; get deterministic hooks for time and randomness; review pull requests for testability, not only for test code. Cite the on-call work — reading logs alongside developers is the same collaboration muscle.

Tell me about a time you found a critical defect late.

Use a real situation, state the impact plainly, then focus on what changed in the process afterwards. Avoid blame, and avoid numbers you cannot support.
