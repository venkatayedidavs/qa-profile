When someone asks how long you have used Playwright, use four beats, and keep every beat true to your own work.

First, state the real scope in one calm sentence. Not the scope you wish you had.

Second, give a concrete artefact straight away. The framework, what it covers, how it runs in the pipeline, and one hard problem you actually solved in it.

Third, bridge to the longer arc. Test design, risk, test management, API testing, and the other tools you have shipped are why a transfer can be fast. Use your arc.

Fourth, close on learning that has a name. Aria snapshots, sharded reporting, or whatever you are actually practising. Specific curiosity is credible. Saying you love learning new things is not.

If you have read about a feature and have not used it, say exactly that, then say how you would apply it. Do not claim the tool, the version, or the feature.

This plan's written example mentions nine years and a particular resume. That example is not your biography. The four beats are what you reuse.
